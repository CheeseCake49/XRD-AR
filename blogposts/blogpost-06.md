# Troubleshooting, Breakthroughs, and Our First Successful Serves  

Today was a long and unexpectedly chaotic day in the XR lab. Since we missed a full work session last week due to the room being booked, we arrived early and ready to be productive. Unfortunately, the universe had different plans for the first half of the day — today was a hardware-nightmare.  

---

## Refining the World  

We began with small but important improvements to our padel environment. The fence finally started looking like an actual fence instead of placeholder geometry, and the net was updated to better resemble a real padel net.  

We also implemented our first version of the **padel racket**, added more environmental details, and started preparing for a proper test inside the Meta Quest headset. On paper, this should have been a smooth start.  

Instead, chaos.  

Every time we tested, the Meta-world behaved strangely. Objects were flickering of place, interactions didn’t work properly, and basic functionality seemed broken. For almost **four hours**, nothing worked the way it was supposed to. It was hard to tell whether the problem was software, the project setup, or the hardware itself — but the frustration level in the room was definitely rising.  

---

## The Mystery Fix  

After lunch, something changed.  
The Meta Quest headset had been charging for a while, and once we tried again, everything suddenly started working normally.  

We still don’t know the exact reason.  
Maybe the headset needed more battery.  
Maybe low power caused tracking glitches or runtime issues.  
Maybe it was just Meta Quest magic.

Whatever the reason, the difference was night and day.  

---

## Real Progress — Fast  

With the hardware behaving, everything else moved quickly:  

- **Ball physics were implemented**, allowing us to finally begin experimenting with realistic behaviour.  
- We created a **button for spawning new balls**, making testing cycles far quicker.  
- We refined **key bindings** and general input interactions.  
- We began tackling the biggest challenge so far — the ball sometimes **passing through the racket** due to collision issues.  

With some guidance from the teacher, we adjusted the physics settings correctly, and that solved most of the problem.  

By the end of the session, we were able to perform actual **padel serves in VR**. Not perfectly realistic yet, but undeniably functional. Seeing the ball bounce off the racket — rather than clip through it — was a huge moment.  

---

## A Bittersweet Ending  

In the final hour and a half, we accomplished more than we had in the previous five hours combined. From complete technical deadlock to hitting consistent serves, today was a rollercoaster.  

The physics still need tuning — especially when it comes to speed, spin, and impact realism — but we finally have the foundation we’ve been working toward.  

Tomorrow, we push for accuracy.  
Today, we’re just relieved it all finally worked.
